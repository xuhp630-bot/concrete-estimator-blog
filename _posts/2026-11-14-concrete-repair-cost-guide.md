# Concrete Repair Cost: When to Fix and When to Replace

Cracked, pitted, or uneven concrete is frustrating — but it doesn't always mean a full replacement. Knowing when to repair vs. replace can save thousands of dollars. Here's a practical guide to concrete repair costs in 2026.

## Repair vs. Replace: The Decision Framework

The right choice depends on how much of the concrete surface is damaged:

- **Less than 25% damaged** → Repair is usually cost-effective
- **25-50% damaged** → Consider extent and type of damage
- **More than 50% damaged** → Replacement typically wins on value

Structural damage (heaving, settlement, undermining) almost always requires replacement, regardless of area affected.

## Common Repair Costs

| Repair Type | Cost Range | DIY Option |
|-------------|-----------|------------|
| Surface crack filling | $3-10/lin ft | Yes |

| Settling/lifting (mudjacking) | $3-6/sq ft | No |

## Surface Repairs: What's Involved

**Crack filling** is the simplest and cheapest repair. For cracks narrower than 1/4 inch, flexible polyurethane sealant works well. Wider cracks need a cementitious patching compound.

**Spalling** (surface flaking/pitting) often comes from freeze-thaw damage or de-icing salt use. Repair involves cleaning the area, applying a bonding agent, and patching with concrete overlay mix.

**Resurfacing** covers the entire existing slab with a thin layer (3/8 to 1 inch) of fresh concrete. It's ideal when spalling is widespread but the structural integrity is sound.

## Structural Repairs: Mudjacking and Lifting

If sections of your driveway, patio, or sidewalk have settled (sunk) or heaved (risen), mudjacking or polyurethane foam lifting can often fix this without full replacement.

Mudjacking pumps a slurry mixture beneath the slab to lift it back to grade. Cost: $3-6 per sq ft, far cheaper than the $6-12/sq ft to remove and pour new concrete.

## When Replacement Makes More Sense

Repair doesn't always win. Replace concrete if:
- The slab is structurally compromised (severely cracked, broken through)
- Soil issues caused the original failure (poor compaction, erosion)
- You want to change the design or layout
- The slab is more than 30 years old with widespread deterioration

## DIY vs. Professional Repair

Small crack filling and patching are genuinely DIY-friendly. Surface resurfacing requires more skill — the mix consistency and timing matter. Mudjacking, foam lifting, and large structural repairs need professional equipment.

For surface repairs, here's what a DIYer typically spends vs. professional work:

| Repair | DIY Cost | Professional Cost |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| Crack fill (20 lin ft) | $20-50 | $200-500 |
| Spalling patch (50 sq ft) | $50-150 | $400-800 |
| Resurfacing (200 sq ft) | $200-400 | $800-2,000 |
