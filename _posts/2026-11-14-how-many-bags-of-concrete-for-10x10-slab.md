One of the most common questions from DIYers tackling a backyard project: exactly how many bags of concrete do I need for a 10x10 slab?

The answer depends on two key variables — the thickness of the slab and the bag size you're using. Let me break it down clearly.

## The Quick Math

A 10x10 slab at 4 inches thick requires **0.37 cubic yards** of concrete (100 sq ft × 0.333 ft = 33.3 cubic feet = 1.23 cubic yards... wait, let me correct that — 100 sq ft × 4/12 ft = 33.3 cubic feet ÷ 27 = **1.23 cubic yards**).

For 60 lb bags: approximately **66 bags**
For 80 lb bags: approximately **49 bags**
For 90 lb bags: approximately **44 bags**

Always buy 10% extra for waste and uneven subgrade.

## Why Bag Size Matters More Than You Think

The difference between 60 lb and 80 lb bags isn't just weight — it's about yield per bag. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 cubic feet, while a 60 lb bag yields about 0.45 cubic feet.

If you're doing the project solo, 60 lb bags are easier to handle. If you have help, 80 lb bags mean fewer trips to the store. ## Delivery vs. Bagged: The Break-Even Point

For a standard 10x10 slab, bagged concrete is usually more cost-effective. Ready-mix delivery has minimum order requirements (typically 1 yard) and delivery fees that add up quickly for small jobs.

However, if your project exceeds 1 cubic yard, check the [concrete delivery cost calculator](https://concreteestimatorhub.com/concrete-delivery-cost-calculator) — the math often shifts in favor of delivery for larger pours.
