# Concrete Footing Cost: Everything You Need to Know Before Digging

Footings are the hidden foundation of decks, fences, retaining walls, and home additions. Getting them right matters far more than most homeowners realize — a failed footing means a leaning deck or cracked wall years later. Here's what concrete footings cost and how to plan your project.

## What Are Concrete Footings?

A concrete footing (sometimes called a foundation footing) is a widened section of concrete at the base of a wall, column, or post. Its job is to distribute structural load across a wider area of soil, preventing settling and shifting.

Common applications:
- Deck posts (required by most building codes)
- Fence posts in areas with frost heave
- Shed foundations
- Home additions and retaining walls
- Structural columns

## Footing Cost Per Linear Foot

The cost of continuous footings is typically measured per linear foot. Factors that move the price:

- **Width and depth** — standard is 16" wide × 8" deep; structural applications go wider/deeper
- **Rebar requirements** — building codes typically require specific rebar sizes and spacing
- **Soil conditions** — unstable or expansive soils need deeper footings
- **Frost depth** — in cold climates, footings must go below the frost line (varies by region)

For planning continuous footing costs, the [concrete footing cost per linear foot](https://concreteestimatorhub.com/blog/concrete-footing-cost-per-linear-foot) guide breaks down costs by region and application.

## Frost Depth: The Hidden Variable

This is where many homeowners underestimate costs. In northern climates, frost depths can reach 4-5 feet, meaning your footing needs to go that deep to prevent frost heave.

A 48-inch deep footing contains significantly more concrete than a 12-inch deep footing — and costs 3-4× as much per unit. Check your local building department for the required frost depth before budgeting.

## DIY vs. Professional Installation

**DIY footing:** Feasible for small post footings (deck posts, fence posts). Tools needed: post hole digger or auger rental ($75-150/day), tube form, concrete bags.

**Professional footings:** Recommended for foundation work, continuous footings, and anything structural in a home addition or new construction.

DIY cost breakdown for a simple post footing:
- Tube form (12" × 48"): $5-8
- Concrete (80 lb bags): 2-3 bags × $8 = $16-24
- Rebar: $5-10
- **Total per footing: ~$30-45**

Professional cost: $100-250 per footing installed.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Going too shallow:** Always account for local frost depth, not just what looks "deep enough."

**Skipping rebar:** Building codes require specific reinforcement. Skipping it can mean failed inspections and redoing the work.

**Wrong diameter:** Post footings should be sized per load tables in your local building code.

**Not testing soil:** Soft, wet, or organic soils may need gravel base layers or deeper footings.
